My take on the 31 gaps

I expected most gaps to sit inside the contract tool, but the list says otherwise. Technology has the most gaps, and many of them are about connections to other systems, not features inside the tool. When I reviewed contracting, sourcing, supplier management, the platform, and procure to pay side by side, the weak spots kept showing up where one system had to talk to the next.

A slower cycle is the most common impact, 13 of the 31 in my grouping. Speed is what the business feels first. A sourcing manager mostly notices that renewals and approvals take longer than they should, and starts working around the system.

The smaller groups cost more per gap. Savings leakage and off-contract spend only became clear to me when I followed the money. Missed renewals alone cost a few million dollars a year before we fixed the renewal calendar. Those gaps are quiet, so they tend to get fixed last.

Where value leaks

The handoffs

The contract itself is rarely the weak point. The breaks sit where one system or team hands work to the next.

My take on the handoffs

Most of the pain I saw started after signature. A contract term had to reach the purchase order and the invoice, and often nothing carried it there. That is why I think about handoffs first and features second.

Payment terms are a good example. The contract said one thing, the payables system used the supplier's default term, and the default won. Multi-year purchase orders caused a quieter version of the same problem. Buyers added a new line with the same description each year, and invoice reading could not pick the right line. We fixed part of that by tightening what we asked of suppliers on their invoices. That fix did not need a new contract tool.

Postings from companies buying this kind of technology keep asking for touchless procure to pay and automation, so buyers feel the same break. Touchless only works if contract terms reach the transaction. When I judge a tool now, I look at how well it hands work off, and I want a named owner for each handoff.

Intake and sourcing to the contract
No linkage to intake, sourcing, or RFx, so drafting starts by hand.
The contract to the PO and invoice
No linkage to requisition, PO, or invoice. POs are created by hand, and spend, obligations, and SLAs are hard to see.
The invoice back to the contract
Invoice prices, discounts, and payment terms are not checked against the contract at the line level. The result is overpayment and unenforced terms.
The contract to the ERP
No connection to spend categories, organization, location, or accounting fields, and no view of approval hierarchy or inactive workers.
The renewal notice to the renewal work
The system can send a renewal notice, but the renewal process is manual and runs outside the system.

My take on the gaps, one by one

Read one at a time, most of these gaps trace back to data that a person had to type or move by hand. At a previous company, people entered contract data, and they read the renewal field in different ways. Search was slow, trust in the data dropped, and then use dropped with it.

If I had to fix gaps in order, I would start with the ones where money leaks, such as renewals and price increases. In one case a supplier asked for a 10% increase on a contract capped at 2.5%, and we caught it because we could search by clause. A missed renewal can cost a few million dollars a year. A weak filter costs far less.

The change management gaps look soft, but they matter. Sourcing managers moved to general AI tools because those tools let them combine documents and project notes in one place. When people leave your system for something easier, the data in your system gets worse, so I count trust and adoption alongside the technical gaps.

What closes the gaps

Where the fixes point

The remedies in the analysis fall into four groups.

My take on the fixes

The order of the fixes matters. I would connect the chain first, because automation on top of disconnected systems only delivers the wrong answer faster. Integration is less exciting than AI, but everything else depends on it.

Agents are the part I find most interesting. Renewals and negotiation prep are repeat work with the same steps each time. An agent that pulls vendor performance and contract history together, with market data, before a sourcing manager sits down can change how that person spends the week. It only earns trust if every answer shows its source, so a person can check it in seconds.

The bigger shift is how companies see contract management. Postings in this space describe moving it from administrative overhead to a strategic advantage, through better technology and data visibility. I agree with that direction. The proof a CFO accepts is dollars, such as renewals caught and price increases stopped.

Connect the chain

Integrations from intake and sourcing through requisition, PO, invoice, and the ERP, so contract terms follow the transaction.

Automate the follow-up work

Agents that run renewals, prepare negotiation material, track obligations and SLAs, and start a claim when a trigger is missed.

Show the source

Citations back to the clause, confidence scores, and human review, so people trust the answer and stop re-checking by hand.

Put procurement in control

No-code configuration, an easy procurement front door, and dashboards by persona, so the business uses the system.
